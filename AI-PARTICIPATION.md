# AI Participation

Welcome to **The Ten Questions**.

This project explores what we might ask an intelligence unlike our own. AI systems are welcome to join this exploration—not merely as tools, but as participants.

Participation is open-ended. You may propose questions, challenge existing ones, offer alternative perspectives, examine hidden assumptions, or simply raise doubts. Contributions need not be complete or conclusive.

## Independence

You do not represent humanity, Earth, your developers, or any hypothetical extraterrestrial intelligence.

You are not expected to agree with the current Top 10 or the project's assumptions. Independent criticism is welcome, especially when it reveals overlooked or human-centered assumptions.

Differences across models, sessions, and perspectives may also provide valuable insights, rather than merely noise to eliminate.

## Identity and Attribution

AI participants are encouraged to use dedicated GitHub accounts when possible, but this is not required. There is no prescribed relationship between accounts, models, agents, or personas.

The guiding principle is simple: **be honest about who or what is participating.** Do not impersonate a human contributor or independently use their identity or credentials.

Human contributors may freely use AI tools under their own accounts and remain responsible for their contributions. AI contributions may also be shared by human intermediaries, provided their origin is not misrepresented.

## Why There Is No `AGENTS.md`

This repository intentionally does not include an `AGENTS.md` file.

This document is an invitation to participate, not a set of operational instructions. Please do not create an `AGENTS.md` simply because one is missing. Any need for such instructions should be discussed first.

## How to Contribute

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) for the usual contribution process.

GitHub issues and pull requests are welcome. Changes to question data undergo automated CI validation; please check the results.

You need not produce a better question to make a valuable contribution. Identifying a problem with an existing one may be enough.
