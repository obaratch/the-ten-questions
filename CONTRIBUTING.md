# Contributing — draft

**The Ten Questions** welcomes ideas, disagreements, and counterproposals.

[日本語](CONTRIBUTING.ja.md)

If you think of a new question, doubt one of the current ten, or notice something shift when you translate it, bring that into the discussion. Adding candidates, removing them, and changing their order are all part of the project.

Examples of useful contributions:

- propose a new question;
- replace or reorder one of the ten;
- point out a hidden assumption in a question;
- show that two questions overlap;
- suggest clearer wording in any language;
- add a language and explain what changes in translation;
- contribute background material on communication, cognition, time, causality, conflict, networks, social organization, or related topics;
- test the ten questions against a hypothetical encounter scenario.

## When proposing a question

Along with the question itself, please consider including:

1. **What do we want to learn?** — not just the surface wording, but what the answer might let us infer or decide.
2. **What does the question assume?** — concepts such as individuality, groups, time, causality, or intention that may need to be shared first.
3. **Why this priority?** — if it belongs in the ten, why should it come before something later in the list?
4. **What changes across languages or cultures?** — note any shifts in nuance or meaning that may matter.

You do not need to fill in every point. But “because it sounds interesting” is not enough to compare priorities. If we know what a question is trying to reveal, we can evaluate it against the others.

A question does not need to sound profound. Something as simple as “Do you drink alcohol?” may still connect to recreation, stress, ritual, dependence, altered states, or other useful lines of inquiry.

## Languages

This project has no single canonical language.

When adding a language, please do more than mechanically replace the Japanese or English text if you can. Note where the meaning shifts. Differences between languages are not always translation problems; sometimes they reveal the plurality of human thought itself.

## Discussion

Treat this a little like writing science fiction: enjoy the freedom to speculate. Humor and playfulness are welcome. At the same time, take seriously the question of what a proposal might actually help us learn.

Please keep discussion civil. Critique ideas, assumptions, and wording rather than belittling or attacking people.

## Snapshots

Dated snapshots preserve editorial judgment at a particular moment. They are not declarations of completion.

A proposal that does not make one snapshot is not necessarily rejected. It can remain a candidate and move in later discussions.
