# Contributing — draft

The Ten Questions is meant to be argued with.

[日本語](CONTRIBUTING.ja.md)

Useful contributions include:

- proposing a new question;
- replacing or reordering one of the ten;
- identifying a hidden assumption in a question;
- showing that two questions overlap;
- improving a formulation in any language;
- adding a language and explaining where translation changes the problem;
- contributing background material on communication, cognition, time, causality, conflict, networks, social organization, or related fields;
- stress-testing the ten questions against a hypothetical encounter scenario.

## When proposing a question

Please include, where possible:

1. the question itself;
2. what we really want to learn from it;
3. what concepts must already be shared for the question to make sense;
4. why it deserves its proposed priority;
5. what a surprising answer might reveal.

A question does not need to sound profound. Compact, playful, or ordinary questions are welcome if they reveal something important.

## Languages

No human language is canonical.

A new language version should not be treated as a mechanical translation task. Please note ambiguities, grammatical assumptions, distinctions that disappear, and distinctions that become newly visible in that language.

Divergence between languages can be useful evidence about the concepts hidden in a question.

## Tone

Be rigorous without demanding solemnity. Critique assumptions rather than people. Curiosity is preferred to certainty.

The project is speculative by nature; clearly separate established knowledge, plausible inference, and imaginative possibility.

## Snapshots

Dated snapshots are editorial milestones, not claims of completion. A proposal that misses one snapshot may remain a candidate for the next.
