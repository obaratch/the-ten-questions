# If We Had More Time

Questions outside the current ten are not discarded. Some are too specific, too dependent on unestablished concepts, too playful, or simply displaced by higher-priority questions. They remain visible because the rejected and unresolved questions are part of the project.

The formulations below are deliberately loose. The interesting part is often the question behind the question.

## Do you get drunk?

**あなた（たち）は酔いますか。**  
**Do you get drunk?**

What we really want to know: whether discomfort, stress, boredom, ritual, pleasure, altered states, or social bonding are sometimes handled through temporary experience rather than permanent solutions. If something analogous exists, does it become recreation, ceremony, vice, medicine, or all of them?

## Do you possess weapons of mass destruction?

**大量破壊兵器を持っていますか。**  
**Do you possess weapons of mass destruction?**

What we really want to know: whether technological capability has created civilization-scale self-destructive power, how that threshold was crossed, and what institutions or norms emerged in response. The concept of a "weapon" may itself fail to map cleanly.

## When was your last war?

**最後に戦争をしたのはいつですか。**  
**When was your last war?**

What we really want to know: whether organized conflict between large social units exists or existed, what ended it, and whether peace is an equilibrium, a temporary condition, or a category that makes no sense to them.

## Do you have an Internet?

**あなた（たち）にはインターネットがありますか。**  
**Do you have an Internet?**

What we really want to know: whether a persistent, civilization-scale network exists; whether it is centralized or a network of networks; and whether increased connectivity produced integration, fragmentation, new localities, or the disappearance of old ones.

Communication with us does not answer this. A counterpart may possess an excellent point-to-point channel without anything resembling a shared public network.

## Do you ever think, “young people these days…”?

**「最近の若い者は」と思うことがありますか。**  
**Do you ever think, “young people these days…”?**

What we really want to know: whether generations exist as social categories, whether culture changes across them, and whether transmission reliably creates friction between old and new participants. This question requires something like age, succession, or generations to have been established first.

## What do you need most, and what do you have too much of?

**今いちばん必要なものは何ですか。そして、いちばん余っていて困っているものは何ですか。**  
**What do you need most, and what do you have too much of?**

What we really want to know: the counterpart's structure of scarcity and surplus. The answers need not be material: energy, computation, information, space, time, attention, population, entropy sinks, or categories we have not imagined may matter more.

If exchange is possible, this may also be the beginning of trade. If exchange is impossible, it is still a compact way to learn what constrains a civilization.

## Other strong candidates

- **Do you want to continue this conversation?** — consent and willingness to sustain contact.
- **Did you know about us before this encounter?** — distinguish prior observation from first direct contact.
- **Do you intend to change anything here?** — intervention without assuming that change is harmful.
- **How do you understand us?** — extremely valuable, but difficult to fit into a yes/no bootstrap.
- **What should we not do next?** — practical and safety-oriented, but depends heavily on shared concepts of action, consequence, and prohibition.

---

A candidate is not a failed question. It is a question whose current priority, formulation, or assumptions have not earned one of the ten slots.
